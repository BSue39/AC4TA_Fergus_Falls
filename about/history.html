Our History

A Century Of Arts, Community, & Preservation

Discover the story of how a historic theater became one of west-central Minnesota's most treasured cultural destinations.

Explore Our History

Preserving The Past. Inspiring The Future.

From its beginnings as a historic downtown theater to its evolution as a thriving arts center, our story is one of preservation, community support, and artistic growth.

Key Milestones

1921

The OrpheumTheatre Opens

The building opens as the Orpheum Theatre, featuring a stage, fly loft, dressing rooms, and approximately 200 seats. Vaudeville acts and live performances become a centerpiece of entertainment in downtown Fergus Falls.

1952

A New Era as the Fergus Theatre

The building undergoes major renovation. The stage and dressing rooms are removed, seating expands dramatically, and the theatre transitions into a modern movie house serving the region.

1970s

Closure & Uncertainty

As television becomes increasingly popular, attendance declines and the theatre eventually closes, leaving the future of the historic building uncertain.

1980s

Community Volunteers Step Forward

The Old Tyme Variety Players begin using the space and lay the groundwork for what would eventually become A Center for the Arts. Community members recognize the building's importance and begin preservation efforts.

1982

The Theatre Is Saved

Gordy and Joan Bakken donate the building. A thrust stage is constructed and the theatre begins its transformation from movie house to performing arts venue.

1994

The Wurlitzer Arrives

Installation of the Geneva Eschweiler Theatre Pipe Organ begins, adding one of the region's most unique musical attractions and helping establish a strong theatre-organ tradition in Fergus Falls.

1995

Grand Reopening

Following a community fundraising effort exceeding $1 million, the theatre reopens as a fully renovated, year-round arts facility with updated accessibility, backstage areas, and audience amenities.

1999

Theatre Organ Community Grows

The local theatre organ chapter is officially organized, helping preserve and promote the Mighty Wurlitzer through concerts, education, and community outreach.

2013

Auditorium Modernization

The auditorium receives new seating, improved sightlines, refreshed finishes, and upgraded patron amenities to enhance the audience experience.

2020-2021

Adapting Through the Pandemic

During the COVID-19 shutdown, AC4TA uses the closure period to renovate the lobby, box office, and restrooms, preparing the facility for future growth.

Today

A Regional Arts Destination

AC4TA serves thousands of visitors annually through touring performances, community theatre, youth programs, arts education, concerts, film screenings, and special events.
